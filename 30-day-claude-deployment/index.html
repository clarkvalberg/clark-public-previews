Access is not deployment.

Buying seats gives people a tool. It does not tell them where Claude belongs in their work, what company information is safe to use, or how to produce a reliable result twice.

Deployment means the behavior changed. People use Claude on real work, inside clear rules, through workflows the company can see and improve.

Thirty days. Four operating moves.

The assessment runs inside the deployment. Your people get practical capability now; leadership gets the evidence and backlog to decide what comes next.

Questions leadership usually asks.

Is this training or implementation?

Both, but the unit of work is deployment. Training happens inside configured workflows, with governance and measurement around it. The goal is not that people understand Claude. The goal is that Claude becomes a useful, repeatable part of how the company works.

Does every employee need the same program?

No. Everyone receives a common operating foundation. Role-based sessions then focus on the work, risks, and patterns relevant to each function. Executives, operators, sales, finance, and product should not receive the same workshop.

What happens to the opportunity backlog?

The company owns it. Leadership can assign items internally, engage another partner, or continue with Transformative through a separate build-and-deploy cadence. The 30-day engagement stands on its own.

Can you promise the whole team is “AI native” in 30 days?

No serious operator should promise a permanent culture change in a month. We can promise a governed environment, role-specific working sessions, deployed workflows, measured evidence, and internal owners. That is enough to move the company from access to operating capability.

The visible change is the product. The assessment is how we find what comes next.
